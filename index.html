🎞️ Your First Project in Five Minutes

If you want to prove to yourself that the program works before investing time in a real project, try this simple exercise.

1. Start a new project

When Shotcut opens, you are already in an untitled project. Give it a name from the "New Project" panel or simply begin — you can save it later with a proper name.

2. Bring in a video

Click "Open File" in the toolbar and choose any video on your computer. It will appear in the playlist panel and also load into the preview window.

3. Put it on the timeline

Drag the clip from the playlist down to the timeline at the bottom of the screen. This is the single most important action in video editing: everything else is done from here.

4. Cut out a section

Move the playhead to where you want a cut, then press the split button (or the S key). Select the piece you do not want and press Delete. The remaining clips close the gap.

5. Export

Click "Export" in the top toolbar, choose a preset such as YouTube or H.264, then click "Export File". Choose a folder and wait for the progress bar to finish. That is a complete video.

Once you have done this once, every other feature in Shotcut is simply an extension of the same idea: pick a clip, place it on the timeline, adjust it, export.

🛠️ Tips for a Smooth First Experience

  • Keep your footage on the same drive. Editing is much smoother when your project file and your video files live on the same disk.
  • Save early, save often. Use File > Save and give your project a clear name before you start cutting.
  • Use proxy files for heavy 4K footage. Shotcut can create lightweight stand-ins so the timeline responds instantly. The export still uses your original files.
  • Learn three shortcuts. Space plays and pauses, S splits the clip at the playhead, and I and O set in and out points. These alone will save you hours.
  • Export last. Do not export until the timeline looks right. Exporting takes time, and it is frustrating to redo it after a small fix.
  • Keep a backup of your original files. Never delete your source footage after a project is finished.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is Shotcut really free?

Yes. Shotcut is free and open source software. There is no subscription, no watermark, no time limit, and no premium tier hiding the good features.

Do I need to install anything else first?

No. All the codecs and libraries Shotcut needs are included with the download. You do not need to install FFmpeg separately.

Windows warned me about the file. Is that a problem?

That message appears for many programs downloaded outside the Microsoft Store. It is a generic reputation warning, not a virus alert. Click "More info" and then "Run anyway" to continue.

Can I edit videos from my phone?

Yes. Copy the files from your phone to your computer, then open them in Shotcut just like any other video. Most phone formats are supported directly.

Can I use it on a Mac or Linux machine?

Shotcut is cross-platform, so versions exist for macOS and Linux as well. This guide focuses on Windows, but the editing workflow is identical on every system.

Where do I get help if I am stuck?

The release page and the project's documentation are the best starting points. Most beginner questions — how to cut, how to add text, how to export for YouTube — are answered by simply exploring the panels in the program.
